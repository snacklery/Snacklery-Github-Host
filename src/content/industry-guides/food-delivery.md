## H1: Edible Cutlery for Food Delivery: A Complete Implementation Guide

### Introduction

Food delivery and cloud kitchens are the final industry in this series, and the most operationally distinct: there's no dine-in service at all, food is packed and transported before consumption, and the business often has no direct interaction with the end customer beyond the delivery itself. That changes several of the assumptions built into earlier guides in this series — extended transit time before consumption is the default, not an edge case, and packaging efficiency matters as much as the cutlery product itself.

**Best products for this industry:** Edible sporks as the default recommendation, given delivery's typically mixed, unpredictable food types and the operational benefit of a single SKU rather than choosing between spoon and fork per order — see our [Edible Sporks Guide](/learn/product-guides/edible-sporks).

### Transit time and packaging considerations

This is the defining operational difference between food delivery and every other industry in this guide series. In dine-in service, cutlery is used within minutes of being stocked or served. In delivery, the food (and any included cutlery) may sit in transit for anywhere from 15 minutes to well over an hour, depending on distance, traffic, and platform logistics — and that time isn't spent in controlled storage conditions the way pre-service airline or railway catering is, but in a delivery bag exposed to variable temperature and humidity.

### FAQ

**How should delivery cutlery be packaged?**
Packaged to protect from moisture and temperature exposure during transit — consider separate moisture-barrier packaging if transit times or delivery-bag conditions are variable.
